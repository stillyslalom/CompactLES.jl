Ablation drive from a hohlraum code

CompactLES has no radiation transport, laser deposition, plasma equation of state or ablation physics. To run the capsule-only step of LLNL's two-step method (an integrated hohlraum calculation gives the radiation drive on the capsule, which then drives capsule-only implosions), it takes the drive as a source term. AblationDrive is the first, deliberately reduced, stage of that coupling: a rocket closure. The hohlraum step is Briareus.jl, a view-factor code, whose CapsuleDrive hands over the capsule's radiation temperature Tr(t), the capsule-averaged incident flux, its Legendre ratios aₗ/a₀ (l ≤ 8, P2 > 0 pole-hot) on a uniform 50 ps grid, the radius the flux was evaluated on, and provenance.

Coupling in memory

Both codes are Julia, so the drive passes in memory, through a package extension, rather than through a file. CompactLESBriareusExt (in this package, loaded when Briareus is) adds one method, radiation_drive(::Briareus.CapsuleDrive), which reads the plain-data CapsuleDrive into an AblationDrive:

using Briareus, CompactLES
history = drive_history(model, op, beams_at, times; lmax = 8)   # Briareus
cd = capsule_drive(history, model)                              # Briareus → plain data
drive = radiation_drive(cd; peak = 145.0, driver = 3)           # CompactLES source
problem = Problem(...; metric = SphericalMetric(), sources = (drive,))

The extension lives here rather than in Briareus because the code it holds is CompactLES physics (a source term, its calibration, and later the radiation boundary condition), and it evolves with this solver; the Briareus side is a stable data type. Neither package depends on the other: AblationDrive takes plain vectors, so any hohlraum code (or a table) can drive it, and Briareus never loads MPI or KernelAbstractions. A file format (HDF5) for non-Julia consumers can follow as a second extension.

The stage-0 model

The closure is the rocket model of indirect drive. With T the drive temperature in heV, the ablation pressure and mass ablation rate are

\[P_\text{abl} = P_0\,T^{3.3} \left(\frac{t}{1\,\text{ns}}\right)^{-0.09}\ \text{Mbar}, \qquad \dot m = 3\times10^5\,T^{3}\ \text{g cm}^{-2}\,\text{s}^{-1},\]

with carbon's subsonic exponents from constant-temperature slab simulations (Trickey, Walsh and Pasley, HEDP 44, 100995 (2022)) and Lindl's mass ablation rate (Lindl et al., PoP 11, 339 (2004), Eq. 6-3), whose prefactor is the default rate. The NIF example below calibrates rate to 2.85×10⁵, keeping the T³ scaling. The pressure prefactor is not published; ablation_pressure_scale sets it from the peak pressure, 145 Mbar for HYBRID-E, and the foot is then checked against HYBRID-E's first-shock requirement, more than 12 Mbar at about 150 eV.

The angular structure enters through the local temperature, $T_\text{eff}^4 = T_r^4\,(1 + \sum_l (a_l/a_0) P_l(\cos\theta))$, on a spherical (r, θ) grid; a one-dimensional run takes the angle average.

Where it acts. An Eulerian code has no ablation surface. Along each radial line the source finds the front as the mean radius of the ablator's outer density gradient, weighted by ρₐ (−∂ρₐ/∂r)⁺, and acts on the ablator mass in a Gaussian layer of width layer about it. Both weights are normalized by per-line sums, so the pressure and the ablation rate the shell feels are exact on the grid, whatever the resolution. In the driver-gas form both weights are one-sided: everything beyond the front counts with weight one, the Gaussian applies only inside it (see below for why).

How the pressure is applied. Two forms are implemented:

  • Driver gas (driver = k). The ablated mass is converted, in place, into species k (the outer gas: the hohlraum fill, then the blowoff), and species k's pressure is relaxed to P_abl by an energy source. The driver gas is then a piston at the ablation pressure. This is the form that works: the shell's pressure follows the drive to within a few percent from the foot to the peak. Two details matter. (1) Any ablator beyond the front is converted first (weight ρₐ there). With a two-sided layer the partly converted ablator just outside the front gets no push, because the driver gas around it is held at uniform pressure. It stays behind as the shell accelerates: 22 % of the ablator at 6 ns in the NIF run. That layer then pulls the front marker off the shell, and from about 6 ns the shell stopped ablating. (2) The pressure relaxation, too, acts fully only beyond the front (e^{−x²} inside it), so driver gas converted inside the shell does not heat the shell toward P_abl behind the front.
  • Body force (driver = 0). The force density −P_abl w on the layer, with the ablated mass removed from the problem. On cold ideal-gas ablators the accelerated layer lags the drive (the shell saw 18 Mbar while 117 Mbar was applied) and spikes into a one-cell sheet; the run fails at the rise of the NIF pulse. It is kept for experiments with other equations of state.

Where the source hooks in

compute_rhs! assembles the flux divergence, the metric source terms and the boundary corrections, and then calls add_sources! at the Runge–Kutta stage time, so add_source!(::AblationDrive, solver, dQ, Q, t) sees the stage's conserved state, its primitives (the driver-gas form reads the pressure) and the stage time, and adds to dQ. Each evaluation makes three pointwise passes (front marker, layer weight, the source itself) and three per-line sums.

  • Threads and GPUs. Every pass is a pointwise! body over plain arrays and scalars, so it runs through the same KernelAbstractions path as the other sources. The per-line sums are sum! over the first dimension of device arrays. The drive table is interpolated on the host once per stage and passed as scalars. Not yet run on a device.
  • MPI. The per-line sums are reduced over the radial sub-communicator, so the radius may be split across ranks (the reduction stages through a host copy).
  • Per-line form. SphericalMetric, an unstretched (r, θ) grid and one patch. The binned form below takes the rest.

Geometry: r–z and 3-D

NIF capsule work runs in three metrics: 1-D spherical, 2-D axisymmetric r–z (cylindrical, the hohlraum axis as the grid axis) and 3-D Cartesian. On the last two no grid line is radial, so the per-line search has nothing to sum along. The drive therefore measures everything from the capsule centre (center, on the axis for r–z) and sums over angular bins instead of lines: bins = (nμ, nφ) equal intervals of μ = cos θ and φ, so the bins have equal solid angle. Each point's distance R, its direction (μ, φ) and the components of R̂ in the grid's local basis are geometry, computed once per patch.

  1. Front. The marker g = ρₐ(−∂ρₐ/∂R)⁺, with ∂/∂R = R̂·∇ from central differences along each grid direction (mirrored at an axis, origin, pole or symmetry plane, one-sided at any other block end), gives per bin R_f = Σ g R dV/R² / Σ g dV/R².
  2. Normalization. The layer weight k (the same one-sided or Gaussian weight as on lines, about R_f interpolated at the point) and the kernel a = e^{−x²}/(√π δ) give per bin N = Σ a dV/R² / Σ k dV/R². Weighting by dV/R² makes each bin's sums ∫ … dr per steradian, so w = k N is the per-line k / ∫ k dr: on a 1-D spherical grid the binned form (geometry = :binned) reproduces the per-line source to 1e-14, the calibration's convention included.
  3. Source. R_f and N are interpolated bilinearly between bin centres at each point's direction, and the drive factor T_eff⁴/T_r⁴ is evaluated there: the Legendre sum on r–z (the azimuthal average), every m of the Schmidt harmonics bₗₘ on a 3-D grid (harmonics, Briareus's CapsuleDrive.ylm, through the extension). The body force is −P w R̂ in the grid's components.

Refinement. A patch sees only itself inside add_source!, and a tile of a refined shell band holds a few cells of the front, so the bins cannot be a patch's own. Before a level's right-hand side, at every stage, the solver calls prepare_source!(source, solver, level, states, comm) (a hook in _level_rhs!, entered by every rank of the level, where collectives are allowed), and the drive sums its two passes over all the level's patches and reduces them over the level's communicator; release_source! drops them after the level's right-hand side. Every level carries its own consistent source. A coarse level under the band does too, and its covered nodes are overwritten by the band's. min_layer widens the layer to that many cells on a level whose spacing does not resolve it (the coarse level under a 1 µm band). Shared tile planes count half in each tile's sums. ablation_band(drive; inside, outside, initial, metric) is an AMR(initial = …) predicate for a band about the root level's last R_f(θ, φ); with regrid_interval and tile it follows the shell as it converges.

Device and MPI. The passes are pointwise! bodies; the bin sums (a few thousand bins) are taken on the host, so a device run copies two per-point arrays per pass. Not yet run on a device. The single-patch path reduces over the patch's communicator, the level path over the level's.

The two responses agree to 1–4 % at every time (both dip at 0.4 ns, where the marker crosses a cell): the binned drive is rotation invariant, as the physics is (the response of degree 2 does not depend on m), and the front is pushed in along the hot ±x as it is at the hot poles. The one leak above 0.003 % is into Y₄₂, up to 0.014 % (8 % of the signal at 0.6 ns; comparable to it while it is a few thousandths): the cubic grid's Y₄₀/Y₄₄ imprint times Y₂₂, since Y₂₂² itself feeds only m = 0 and 4. The DT ice is not yet moving (the shock reaches it near 1 ns), its b₂₂ −0.001 %. The spherical run's own imprint, b₄₀ and b₄₄ of R_f (−0.15 % each from 0.5 ns), is the cubic grid's. The Y₂₂ run grows to −0.28 % at 0.9 ns and fails at 0.97 ns, as 1-D does at 1.24 ns on this grid. A 3-D band (60 µm root, 20 µm tiles of 8 root nodes over 0.93 < R < 1.22 mm, bins 16 × 64; 32 × 128 failed at 0.03 ns, one root cell per bin) runs the level path and reproduces the uniform run at 0.1 ns (ablator left 0.9995, front within 0.4 µm), but its covered-node timestep (below) kept it from 0.2 ns in 30 minutes.

Not yet. At 1 µm a full 3-D capsule shell is about 10⁹ cells: 3-D needs the band, and the band's cost is set by two CompactLES limits met here, not by the drive. (1) A root level under a band takes the timestep of its covered nodes, which hold the restricted shell: their artificial diffusivity at the root spacing made the level-0 rate 3865 ns⁻¹ against the band's 958 at 0.15 ns, eight times the steps of the uniform 10 µm grid (subcycling does not help; covered nodes are held to CFL 0.75 in max_rate). (2) Tiled levels are fragile on this problem: r–z tiles of 10 root nodes failed at 0.03–0.08 ns near the axis where tile corners meet (8 and 16 run), and the 8-node run failed at 0.62 ns. A single box (tile = 0) ran stably in the same test. Moving refinement exists (a time-dependent predicate or :sensor, with regrid_interval and tile); ablation_band supplies the predicate, untested over a regrid here. Separately, the multi-D starts want CFL 0.25: from the cold initial state at 0.4, r–z at 15 µm failed near the axis within 0.035 ns with no drive at all.

What stage 0 can and cannot check

The rocket's outputs are its validation: the implosion velocity (HYBRID-E 380–400 km/s), the ablator mass remaining (5–6.6 %) and the coupled kinetic energy (19–33 kJ). With ideal-gas, fully ionized materials and no conduction, shock timing, adiabat, stagnation pressure and bang time are not predictions, and the energy the driver gas receives is not the capsule's absorbed energy.

The NIF drive (2026-10-24, examples/briareus_capsule.jl). A static Briareus model of an N221204-class hohlraum (11.24 × 6.4 mm gold, 3.1 mm entrance holes, the delivered pulse by eye, the mock HYBRID-E pointing on 48 quads, no CBET; Briareus 785684d) gives a capsule Tr peaking at 297 eV. The calibration (peak = 145) sets the pressure prefactor to 4.81 Mbar, so the foot gives 17 Mbar where Tr first reaches 150 eV (more than 12, as required). The capsule is ideal-gas carbon (3.5 g/cc, 1.050–1.135 mm), DT ice and 0.3 mg/cc DT gas, with a 10 mg/cc He driver gas beyond it, on a 1-D grid of 1400 points (1 µm), with a 5 µm layer and the driver-gas form.

Robustness. The first runs, with a 1 mg/cc driver, failed in the foot at about 0.5 ns unless run! was called in 50 ps pieces with retries. Held at P_abl, that driver is a 2–7 keV ideal gas sitting against a 0.05 eV, 3.5 g/cc ablator. From 0.1 ns on, the two or three cells of that contact have negative internal energy. The deficit grows as the foot rises (1 to 5 Mbar between 0.3 and 0.5 ns) until a density goes negative and the timestep collapses. A lower CFL did not help (0.2 failed too). A 10 mg/cc driver is ten times cooler and has a third of the sound speed. With it the foot runs at CFL 0.4 with no retry (so does ArtificialProperties(C_kappa = 0.1), which smears the contact), and the negative-energy cells are then the ordinary ones at the shock in the cold ablator. Its mass is about 1 % of the ablator's. The run is now one run! call to past peak velocity. StepControl(retries = 4) recovers one or two timestep collapses near 7.3 ns, when the first shock converges on the origin through the 0.3 mg/cc gas, as in spherical Noh. The run takes 5–8 minutes on one thread, down from 30 on four. The 2 µm grid now runs too.

The band 5–6.6 % gives A = 2.7–3.0×10⁵, so Lindl's Nova-era 3×10⁵ already sits at its low-mass end. The T³ scaling is kept. Lindl's form ṁ ∝ I^{3/4} is T³, and the rocket's exhaust velocity P/ṁ ∝ T^{0.5} (his Eq. 6-4) with carbon's P ∝ T^{3.3} would give T^{2.8}. Most of the ablator goes in the 290–297 eV peak, so the exponent hardly matters next to the prefactor. With both prefactors fixed, the implosion velocity is the check. It is 328–344 km/s across the band, 10–14 % short of 380–400. The coupled energy, 25–26 kJ, is inside HYBRID-E's 19–33 kJ. Reaching 380–400 km/s takes A ≈ 3.5–3.75×10⁵, which leaves 1.5–2.5 % of the ablator. The rocket cannot meet both targets with this pressure history, so the shortfall is in P/ṁ at fixed mass, not in ṁ. Lindl's ideal rocket, 10⁷ T^{1/2} ln(m₀/m) cm/s, gives 395 km/s at this mass ratio (295 eV; 4.69 mg to 0.47 mg of ablator and DT). The hydrodynamic rocket loses 15 % to a drive that falls from 8 ns, to convergence and to the fuel's pressure. Candidates, all beyond stage 0: the ideal-gas carbon's adiabat (on the 2 µm grid the shell's peak density falls from 18 g/cc at 5 ns to 5.6 g/cc at 7 ns), the 145 Mbar peak, and the gray drive.

The calibration belongs to the drive. Briareus 3f25f29, with the late-time wall-loss history, peaks at 285 eV. Recalibrated to 145 Mbar (prefactor 5.53 Mbar, 19.6 Mbar at 150 eV), the example with the same A gives 307 km/s at 9.12 ns, 8.2 % and 25.6 kJ.

The grid is not converged at this level. At 2 µm the same A gives 298 km/s and 7.3 %, against 336 km/s and 5.7 % at 1 µm, and the 0.5 µm run fails at the rise, 5.5 ns. Read the velocity as ±10 %. Before the one-sided weights, the stranded layer counted as ablator remaining (19.6 % with Lindl's rate, 315 km/s, the first result reported here). That number measured the model's defect, not the ablation rate.

The body-force form failed at 5.1 ns (1 µm grid, before these changes).

r–θ: the drive's P2 history on the shell. The same drive was run with its Legendre ratios through l = 8 (Pabl ∝ (1 + Σ aₗ/a₀ Pₗ)^{3.3/4}) on an annulus 0.25–1.4 mm, 575 × 64 cells (2 µm radial, the full polar range, a slip wall at 0.25 mm, which only matters near stagnation), and A = 2.85×10⁵. The run took about an hour on two threads to 7.6 ns, where it was stopped. The pole-hot foot (P2 > 0 to 4.7 ns) flattens the shell at the poles (oblate, P2 < 0). The pole-cold peak (−0.09 from 5 ns) stops the flattening at 5.5–6 ns and reverses it, and the shell turns prolate near 7.1 ns. That is the swing: a shell shape that changes sign during the implosion, with a lag of about 1 ns after the drive. It is not yet Masse's sign inversion, which is a property of the response G(t, τ) to an impulse at τ (pole-hot before ~4.5 ns gives an oblate hot spot, later a prolate one). Here the drive's own P2 changes sign at 4.7 ns. Separating the two needs impulse runs (the same drive with P2 only before, or only after, a time τ), carried to stagnation on a grid that reaches the origin.

Stage 1 replaces the closure with gray flux-limited diffusion and a Marshak boundary carrying 4F_inc(θ,t)/c, with table equations of state and flux-limited conduction.
